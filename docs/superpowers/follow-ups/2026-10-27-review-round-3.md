# Follow-up: What review rounds 3 and 4 left open

**Date:** 2026-10-27, updated 2026-11-10 after round 4
**Status:** Not planned as standalone tasks. Pick opportunistically, or turn a group into a spec.

## Context

Round 3 of the code-review loop reviewed the 163 round-2 fix commits that shipped in v1.3.0 without a review of their own. Two independent verifiers checked every finding, and nothing below was fixed in that round.

Round 3 fixed everything that was confirmed in scope and estimated at 30 minutes or less:

- 62 correctness defects, each with a regression test. The review ran three passes, and each pass also checked the previous pass's fixes.
- 60 quality changes.
- One test fixture that crashed the interpreter. It fed mock data through typed Qt signals.

Round 4 (2026-11-07 to 2026-11-10) took the 18 defects round 3 had listed as found outside its scope. Two verifiers re-checked each one against v1.4.0: 15 were confirmed, 2 were disputed but reproduced by the fixers, and 1 was refuted. The 17 real defects were fixed test-first, in 17 commits. Three review passes then checked those fixes with the same method: reviewers by area, then two verifiers per finding. They confirmed 46, 24 and 10 problems in the fixes, and everything that both verifiers called worth fixing now was fixed. See [Round 4 update](#round-4-update).

What is left falls into five groups:

- Decisions only the maintainer can make.
- Defects found in code the rounds did not fix.
- Refactors estimated at over 30 minutes.
- Smaller follow-ups the fixers noticed in passing.
- Findings the two verifiers disagreed on.

## Needs a decision

1. **Conflicted hunks** still show a stage checkbox and a discard button that fail with a "hunk ... not found" error in the Operations Log. Hide them, or route them to whole-file staging?
2. **Push remote in fork setups.** Pull follows the branch's upstream; push still targets `origin`. Supporting `pushRemote` / `pushDefault` needs a new port.
3. **Cancelling an interactive rebase** midway: leave the repo REBASING, or abort? A plain rebase now runs in the remote-op slot without a Cancel button. The interactive rebase still shows one, and it stops nothing: a click logs "cancelling..." and the rebase ends as done a few seconds later. Its done row also has no Undo link, unlike the plain rebase's. Hide Cancel for it too, or make it stop the rebase?
4. **Localised git messages.** Run worktree commands under `LC_ALL=C` so the dirty and locked messages still parse?
5. **Graph toolbar overflow.** It is not clipped at the default width, but nothing handles a narrower one.
6. **What to block while a rebase runs.** Round 4 gated the rebase banner's Abort and Continue and the Local + remote button in the Delete tag prompt, and made the toolbar Push and Pull, which a detached HEAD already refused, say a rebase is running. Checkout, commit, reset and stash stay clickable, and a checkout in that window succeeds and makes the rebase fail. One guard shared by all of them, or a check inside each action, as Pull now has?
7. **The main checkout under a linked worktree's own row.** When a linked worktree has a row of its own, the list under it leaves out the main checkout (under the main repo's row, that checkout is the row). Should it be listed?

## Round 4 update

**Refuted: paging can skip commits.** The graph's `_load_more` worker does read `_extra_tips` when it runs, so a branch click in the middle of a page fetches a page of a different walk. But the click's reload sets `_reload_queued` in the same call, and `_on_append_done` drops the stale page before it touches the model, then reloads with the new tips. About 370 randomised interleavings appended nothing wrong; the only cost is one wasted walk. Optional hardening, about 10 minutes: capture `extra_tips` beside `skip` in `_load_more`.

**Not every `exec()` dialog leaks.** A reviewer suspected that Preferences, About, Merge, Reset, Interactive Rebase, Create tag, Identity and the other dialogs the app opens with `exec()` stay under the window until it closes. PySide6 6.12 hands a window-parented dialog back to Python inside the real `QDialog.exec()`, so a dialog survives only while something still references it: a closure that captures `self` and is connected to a signal of its own children, or the closure `connect_widget` connects to the theme manager's `theme_changed` (the Clone dialog is freed by `release()`, the Insight dialog by `WA_DeleteOnClose`). Of the dialogs listed, three had such a closure, and all three are now freed when they close: the Theme dialog (each View > Appearance opening left a dialog of about 150 widgets under the window that every later theme change restyled), the Worktrees dialog and the Checkout Branch dialog. A probe that stubs `exec()` says all of them leak, which is how the reviewer got there.

**The fixes were also run in the real application.** After the second review pass, four exercisers drove the real `main.main()` stack offscreen, each scenario in its own process with a temporary HOME and settings, over real temporary git repositories: linked worktrees, Ctrl+W and removing the worktree on screen; remote operations and the background rebase; the Clone, Add Worktree, Appearance, Worktrees and Checkout Branch dialogs; the diff panes, graph landing and blame. About 115 runs and 28 scenarios gave no crash, no traceback and no `MagicMock` line. Two scenarios failed, and both are items this document already lists as deferred: a RECENT row spelled another way survives a worktree removal, and a late failure of a repeated blame request for the same revision wipes the newer blame. Everything else the round fixed behaved as its tests say.

**What the later passes found in the round's own fixes.** Pass 1 confirmed 46 problems; they are fixed and visible in the history. The three worst of pass 2: Enter in the graph search on the already-current row did not cancel a pending branch landing; Ctrl+W between a worktree removal and the landing of its replacement wrote the deleted worktree into RECENT; and the viewport loader's parentless timer, freed by the garbage collector on a worker thread, killed some forward-order runs of the diff-pane test files in one process (exit 139) (the loader and its timer are now children of the scroll area). Pass 3 found that the repo list's Close item still did what that Ctrl+W did, because the guard sat in one of the two entries that reach `_on_repo_close`; it now sits where they meet. It also corrected the README's Ctrl+W row, which named how a worktree was opened where the code tests whether the worktree has a row of its own.

**What the evidence does not cover.** Every test run and every exerciser scenario ran offscreen on Windows, with macOS message-box titles simulated by a plugin. CI sets `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen` only in its Linux step, so its Windows and macOS jobs use the native platform plugins, and the new tests that run a dialog's real `exec()` open real windows there. Nothing was run on Linux or on a real macOS.

## Defects found outside the round's scope

Each was reported by a reviewer or a fixer in round 4. Most were reproduced in a scratch run; none has a failing test in the repository.

**Threads and object lifetime**

- **Worker signals are still parentless in eight places.** `graph.py` (both load paths), `sidebar.py`, `reflog_pane.py`, `insight_dialog.py`, `remote_op_queue.py` (`_RemoteSignals`), `main_window.py` and the Add Worktree dialog's `_LocationCheckSignals`. When the owner is replaced or destroyed while a worker runs, the worker can drop the last reference and delete the QObject on its own thread. Round 4 fixed this in the diff panes, the blame pane and the Clone dialog: parent the signals to the widget, connect every answer signal to `deleteLater`, wrap the worker's emits in `suppress(RuntimeError)`, and pin it with a test shaped like `test_blame_load_signals_destroyed_on_gui_thread.py`. The reflog pane, the Insight dialog and the Add Worktree dialog are created and destroyed during a session, so do those first; the graph is the hottest, because it reloads on every focus return. About 20 to 35 minutes each with its test. Do these before the self-reference in `connect_widget` (see the disagreements below). The reflog pane's tests wait with `waitSignal` on its parentless signals (`test_reflog_pane.py`, two places); once those signals are deleted with `deleteLater`, such a wait can lose its wake-up, and the tests should use `wait_until_deleted` from `hunk_diff_helpers.py`.
- **A pane destroyed while a worker is inside `emit()` can crash the process.** Since round 4 a load's signals are children of the pane, so destroying the pane in the instant a worker emits deletes the object the worker is calling into. A stress probe on the real `HunkDiffWidget` ended in an access violation at the emit in 3 of its runs. An emit of the same shape sits in `hunk_diff.py` twice, `working_tree.py` twice and `diff.py` once; the blame pane has the same shape and, unlike the diff panes, is closed in normal use. The suite does not show it because the window is the length of one emit. Parenting the signals to `QApplication.instance()` instead closes it (the connection dies with the pane, a late emit finds a live object with no receiver, `deleteLater` still frees it on the GUI thread, and the `suppress` can go; about 40 minutes). Round 4 kept the `suppress` and documents the residual in the code. A separate stress run on the blame pane, closing it at random instants around the emit, survived 1,500 to 6,000 closes at each of three sizes (500, 5,000 and 100,000 lines) without a crash or a warning, so the window is narrow.
- **A failed `Thread.start()` leaves state set.** `remote_op_queue.py` sets `_remote_running` before it starts its thread, so a start that fails wedges the remote slot for the session; `repo_lifecycle.py` leaves a repo request pending; `graph.py` sets `_loading` before it starts either load thread, so every later reload and page load is turned away until a repo switch; the Remote Branches and Submodule dialogs go busy before they start their threads, and a busy dialog refuses to close. Latent: it needs the OS to refuse a thread, when every worker fails anyway. About 10 minutes each (reset in an `except`, or set the state after `start()`). The Clone dialog was fixed.

**Graph**

- **A failed reload leaves its state behind.** `_on_load_failed` keeps `_pending_scroll_oid` and `_extra_tips`. After a failed click on a branch, a later bare reload (the focus-return one) lands on that branch's commit, and a later `_load_more` pages with the new tips and the old skip.
- **A branch click loads one page more than it needs and lands at the bottom edge.** The landing scrolls its target to the top while the scroll bar still has the old range, so `_on_scroll` asks for another page (`_land_on_pending_target` → `scroll_to_oid` → `_load_more`): three graph loads instead of two, 150 rows instead of 100, and the commit sits at the bottom of the viewport. Harmless to correctness.
- **Two short-lived oddities around a pending landing.** While its doubling reload runs, the commit picked earlier has no current row in the list although the diff pane still shows it (one load; longer on a large repo). A search typed while a landing is pending shows the branch tip's diff for a moment before the match takes over; the final state is right.
- **Dropping a landing can lose the picked commit.** When a pick of a drawn commit supersedes a pending branch landing, the load already running comes back as a plain first-round load. If the branch's own commits push the picked commit past the first page, `_restore_view_state` cannot find it and clears the selection while the diff pane still shows the commit. Rare: it needs a branch with more than a page of new commits. Fix: keep the doubling rounds with a "keep this commit drawn" goal; about 45 minutes.

**Remote operations and rebase**

- **Push and Pull after a rebase stops on a conflict give the wrong advice.** HEAD is detached and the repo is REBASING, so the Operations Log says "HEAD is not on a branch (detached, or no commits yet). Check out a branch first"; it should say to continue or abort the rebase.
- **A plain rebase that stops on a conflict is logged as an error.** The row is red, reads "ERROR: Rebasing (1/3) ... hint: ..." with git's raw progress and hint lines (including a `git rebase --skip` hint the UI has no button for), and force-expands the log, although a conflict stop is a normal state with the Rebase in progress banner up.
- **The Rebase in progress banner is unreliable while a background rebase runs.** Seen in the real app with a slow hook: with the same setup the banner came up within half a second in 4 of 9 runs and not at all until the rebase ended (or F5) in the other 5; the working-tree commit button also kept reading "Commit". Abort and Continue are refused while the slot is busy anyway, so only the display lags. A candidate cause, not proven: `RepoChangeDetector` drops watcher events for 500 ms after the window reloads itself (`repo_change_detector.py`, `notify_self_reload`), and the rebase writes nothing else under `.git` during a long hook.
- **Rebase Continue and Abort, and the other local git actions, still run on the GUI thread.** Continue replays the rest of the todo list and freezes the window the way a plain rebase did before it moved to the remote-op slot. Merge, cherry-pick, revert, reset and checkout have the same freeze class; not measured.
- **Re-opening the active repo mid-rebase loses the rebase's Undo.** A click on the active repo's row is ignored, but opening it again (the Open dialog on its folder, Open in the Worktrees dialog, or the Open entry on a linked worktree's row) rebuilds the session buses, and the rule that ties an Undo to its buses then gives that rebase a plain "done" line. A path-based check would restore the link.
- **The Delete tag prompt reads the remote-op slot once.** If the running operation ends while the prompt is open, "Local + remote" stays disabled and the text goes stale. After "Local only" the remote copy cannot be deleted from the UI, because the sidebar lists only local tags, and the prompt does not say so.
- **Cancel and the tag refresh.** A Cancel click in the few milliseconds between a fetch or tag push ending and `ls-remote` starting is lost; there is no op-level cancel flag. `get_remote_tags` uses the adapter's single cancel handle, so a caller that overlapped another remote operation would let Cancel kill the wrong process. `_update_remote_tag_cache` swallows every other refresh failure: an unreachable remote after a successful fetch or tag push leaves a stale cache and no log line. The batch remote-branch delete (`delete_remote_branches`) still cannot be cancelled.

**Worktrees and repo lifecycle**

- **Deleting the branch checked out in the worktree on screen leaves the window on the dead path.** `branch_flows._remove_worktree_for_branch_delete` removes the worktree synchronously on the GUI thread and never reaches `_on_worktree_removed`, so the window keeps showing the folder and its change detector keeps watching it. Read from code and probed; about 45 to 60 minutes.
- **More worktree-removal gaps.** A reload already in flight, or an F5 during the removal, still runs `git status` in the worktree; stopping the detector only removes the reloads it schedules. A window opened on a subfolder of the worktree (`W/src`) is matched by neither the removal fix nor the repo list's text comparison. `forget()` receives only the open row's stored spelling: a RECENT or active entry spelled like git's, next to an open row spelled otherwise, is not forgotten in the same session, and the start-up prune handles it at the next launch. Reproduced in the real app: a worktree opened through the Open dialog and then closed sits in RECENT with forward slashes, is reopened from its child row and removed, and its RECENT row stays, so clicking it tries to open a deleted folder. Forgetting every stored spelling that `same_path_text` matches would close it; about 20 minutes with a test.
- **More GUI-thread disk reads that could hit a dead share.** `repo_lifecycle.py` (`Path(candidate).is_dir()` when it chooses the repo to show after a removal), `right_panel.py` (`os.path.exists` on `<path>/.git`) and the change detector's `exists` / `is_dir` on watched paths; that last one runs on the GUI thread too: the window builds the detector there (at start-up, whenever a repo opens, and when a failed worktree removal puts the watch back) and the detector makes those calls in its constructor. The Add Worktree dialog's per-keystroke check is not debounced: while a dead server blocks one check, each further change starts another daemon thread that blocks too. That is harmless to the UI and bounded by typing.
- **Ctrl+W or Close on a removed worktree's row after a click elsewhere.** The round's guard covers the time between a worktree removal and the landing of its replacement. If the user clicks another row in that window, the pending request becomes a plain switch; Ctrl+W (or Close on the dead row) then no longer sees a replacement and writes the removed folder into RECENT, where it stays until the next launch prunes it. It needs a slow replacement, a click and a keypress inside that window, and only a worktree that had a row of its own is affected. A robust fix keeps the fact that the screen is gone apart from the pending request (set where a replacement is requested for a closed or removed repo on screen, cleared on landing and in `_clear_window`); about 40 to 60 minutes with tests.
- **Open on the stale child row of a removed worktree.** The dead row stays drawn until the replacement lands. Its Open entry (`_on_worktree_action`, which calls `_switch_repo`) replaces the pending request, and the window ends on the deleted folder with "Cannot open ...: Repository not found" and nothing pending. The cause is the same as above: nothing reloads the list between the removal and the landing. Not estimated.

**Branches and upstream**

- **Set Upstream with "(none)" on a branch that has no upstream** calls `unset_branch_upstream`, which errors and shows "Set upstream failed". The dialog should treat it as a no-op.
- **A local branch named like a remote-tracking one still confuses other lookups.** The fix covers `set_branch_upstream`. `reset_branch_to_ref(branch, 'origin/x')` uses `revparse_single`, which resolves a local `origin/x` first. The checkout flow shields it: a name that a local branch also has is checked out as that branch, so the reset it offers never sees such a name. The reset offered after a double-click checkout (`_offer_reset_to_remote_if_diverged`) is not shielded: it passes the branch's upstream shorthand straight in, so beside a local `origin/x` it resets the branch to that local branch's tip, while the prompt names the remote branch. `BranchesDialog._start_point_oid` maps a start point `origin/master` to the local look-alike's tip. The dialog table and `LocalBranchInfo.upstream` print `origin/master` for both. `git branch --set-upstream-to` also writes `branch.<name>.rebase=true` when `branch.autoSetupRebase` applies, which libgit2's setter never did; harmless, because the app pulls with `--rebase`. No test covers set or unset upstream on a bare repository.

**Diff, blame and clone**

- **A failed diff fetch has no in-app log.** The notice reads "Diff could not be loaded." because the old pointer to the log led nowhere: the commit view's fetch failure only reaches the Python logger. A `load_failed` signal through `reload_coordinator` into the Operations Log is the real fix; it must report only real failures, since a path whose change vanished between the file list and the diff query has no entry and is not one. About 45 to 60 minutes.
- **Blame.** A request repeated for the same revision is not covered by the staleness check: if "Blame before X" fails transiently, the user goes Back and repeats it, and the second one succeeds, the first one's late failure still matches the current identity and clears the second one's blame. A per-request token would close it (about 30 minutes). Reproduced in the real app: the newer blame is replaced by an empty editor and the older request's failure message, with the Back button still visible. `_blame_before` also calls `get_commit_detail` on the GUI thread before it starts the worker.
- **The hunk discard button is nearly invisible in the light theme.** `hunk_diff.py` builds each discard button's icon straight from `ic_close.svg` (`_CLOSE_ICON_PATH`) and never tints it from a theme token, unlike the rest of the pane. The dark theme looks fine.
- **View > side-by-side reloads the commit diff,** so every file block the user had collapsed opens again. Probably accepted (the docstring says a full reload).
- **A destroyed commit-detail widget can raise in the event loop.** `commit_detail.py:89` connects the avatar loader's `enabled_changed` to a lambda that calls `self.update()` and never disconnects it. Toggling the Gravatar setting afterwards raises `RuntimeError`; `tests/e2e/conftest.py` works around it.
- **Clone containment is text-only.** A symlink or junction inside Location can still lead a typed name out of it, and a name such as `a/../b` keeps its `..` in the stored path (cosmetic).

**Dialogs and settings**

- **Other `exec()` dialogs are freed only by Python's reference count.** Preferences, About, Worktrees, Add Worktree and Checkout Branch are parented to the window and no closure keeps them alive, so they go when the opener returns. A `findChildren()` call on the window while one is open would pin it (reproduced with PySide6 6.12 alone); nothing in the app does that today. Running every `exec()` dialog through `run_and_discard` would remove the fragility; about 20 minutes. `ThemeDialog` itself still captures `self` in three closures (the swatch and lane button lambdas at `theme_dialog.py:250` and `:304`, and `_snap_typo`), so any new caller must use `run_and_discard`. Removing the cause takes about 40 to 45 minutes: make `_snap_typo` a bound method and give the swatch and lane buttons a small subclass with a `picked` signal instead of lambdas (`functools.partial` does not help, it still holds `self`), and pin it with a real `exec()` on a bare dialog. `WA_DeleteOnClose` is not a drop-in while `appearance.py` runs the dialog through `run_and_discard`: `exec()` deletes the dialog first and the helper's `deleteLater` then raises `RuntimeError` (true of any dialog with that attribute; none is passed to the helper today).
- **Small wording and layout nits in the Theme dialog.** The group "Branches & refs" is drawn as "Branches _refs" (the `&` is a mnemonic prefix; write `&&`), View > Appearance opens a window titled "Theme", and at a 150% text scale the Gravatar checkbox label is clipped at the default width.
- **Add Worktree writes nothing to the Operations Log** when it succeeds, while Clone and Checkout do, and Remove worktree does only for the worktree on screen or one removed along with its branch. The Reflog status reads "1 movements".

**Tests**

- **Test helpers are copied.** `_settled` in `test_closing_a_repo.py` repeats `_quiet` in `test_worktree_removal_lands_somewhere.py`, and the refusal wait is written out four times in the latter. `_shown_text` and the finder for the diff notice repeat across three diff-pane test files, and the new rebase tests copy builders that exist elsewhere. Older tests in `test_closing_a_repo.py` still end with graph workers running.
- **Stray threads and process-wide `Thread` fakes.** About 36 older window tests leave graph workers and `Popen` reader threads running for 25 to 200 ms after the test returns, and 30 patch sites in six test files replace the process-wide `threading.Thread`. A stray `subprocess.communicate` that runs while a fake is installed gets a mock thread (on Windows `communicate` builds its reader threads with `threading.Thread`) and raises `TimeoutExpired`, or adds an entry to the patcher's call list. The smallest failing pair on Windows is `test_closing_a_repo.py::test_ctrl_w_in_a_linked_worktree_while_another_repo_opens_hands_over_to_it` followed by `test_commit_diff_map_race.py`, which fails 3 runs in 30 with `ValueError: too many values to unpack`; a seeded shuffle of 22 window, graph and diff files hit it with 1 seed in 8. The default order CI uses is safe, because the strays are gone before the patchers run. Two fixes: an autouse fixture in `tests/presentation/conftest.py` that joins any thread a test started (about 15 minutes; 0 failures in 40 runs of the pair with it), or scoping the 30 fakes to the module under test with `monkeypatch.setattr('<module>.threading', SimpleNamespace(Thread=...))` (about 45 minutes). Round 4 did this for the graph tests (`fake_graph_threads` in `graph_helpers.py`); `test_graph_signals.py` still patches `threading.Thread` process-wide at eight sites and `test_remote_branches_dialog.py` at five, inside short `with` blocks. What `fake_graph_threads` guarantees is pinned only by real-repository tests, and only on Windows, where `subprocess` builds reader threads; a three-line test asserting that `threading.Thread` is still the real class after it would pin it on Linux and macOS too.
- **Lifecycle pins that are missing.** The `has_row` check in `_request_to_replace_removed` (which asks whether the replacement is already open) is pinned by no test: writing it as `home in open_repos` passes all 65 tests of the seven lifecycle files, although a probe shows today's behaviour is right (a 5-minute test). Likewise `_worktree_group_owner` looks its owner up as text, and `test_the_owner_is_found_however_its_path_is_written` pins that on Windows only: it relaunches onto a linked worktree whose owner row is written with forward slashes and capitals, a spelling that differs from git's only on Windows, so on Linux and macOS an `==` lookup passes it too. Its docstring also says "compared as written" where `_open_row_for` says "matched as text, never `==`". No test picks Close in the real context menu either: the new test emits the list's `repo_close_requested` signal, which covers the wiring, and a one-off probe through `_show_context_menu` agreed.
- **Dialog wiring that no test pins.** The Git menu's Branches, Remote Branches and Submodule dialogs are not pinned to `run_and_discard` (only the Remotes dialog is). The Worktrees dialog's Open, Remove, Lock and Unlock buttons have no real-click test; the wiring is a plain `clicked.connect` like the Add button's, which has one. A parametrised test takes about 10 minutes.
- **An ordering that only a docstring pins.** The diff panes and the blame pane connect the pane's slot before `signals.deleteLater`, and `wait_until_deleted` relies on it. Swapping the two connects in `hunk_diff.py` would make the stale-diff-map test's new assertion pass vacuously, about once in 6,000 runs and only on a run that is already red. A one-line comment at each connect site is the cheap pin; a test would need a deterministic GUI-thread emit.
- **The stale-diff-map test does not join its workers.** On its red path the new worker is released in a `finally` block and runs on through teardown. Wrap the body in `workers_joined_on_exit`.
- **The clone dialog's garbage-collection test** fails only when both the parent and the `deleteLater` connection are removed, so it pins "at least one of the two"; each guard is pinned alone by another of the three clone tests. A harmless overlap.
- **An intermittent `Cannot copy-convert (MagicMock)` line** reaches stderr when `test_a_menu_open_as_another_repo_lands.py` runs with `test_a_repo_opening_mid_confirm.py`. Some test emits a MagicMock through the typed `reload_done` signal in `graph.py`.

## Refactors deferred (over 30 minutes)

Each was confirmed by both verifiers as worth doing, but it was too large for the round or needed changes across several owners.

- **Clone behind a port.** Move `CloneProgress` and `CloneCancelled` to the domain and clone behind an application port. The Clone dialog is the last presentation module that imports infrastructure. About 75 minutes.
- **One cancellable remote branch delete.** Deleting a branch on a remote is implemented twice, in `remote_ops.py` and `branch_ops.py`, and only one of them is cancellable. About 45 minutes.
- **Worktree removal refusals as domain outcomes.** Name them in the domain and keep one override rule. Today presentation dispatches on infrastructure exception class names in two places, with three different force prompts. About 60 minutes.
- **Add Worktree's location rules behind the port.** What `git worktree add` accepts as a location is encoded and probed on disk in the dialog. About 45 minutes.
- **The squash rule in the domain.** Move interactive rebase's "nothing to squash into" rule next to `RebaseAction`. About 20 minutes, deferred with the dialog work above.
- **One owner for theme saves.** Saving appearance and the custom theme as one unit is split between `ThemeManager` and the theme dialog. About 40 minutes.
- **`LogPanel.report_error`.** Replace the expand-then-`log_error` pair repeated at about 47 sites and four wrappers. About 50 minutes.
- **Worktree ownership as window state.** Which repo owns the worktree on screen is kept in the repo-list widget, and the lifecycle rules read it back. About 45 minutes.
- **One background-worker helper.** Move `widgets/background.py` to services and migrate the remaining ~13 widgets that start `threading.Thread` by hand. Round 4 pasted one idiom into six workers (the blame pane, the Clone dialog and four diff-pane paths): signals parented to the owner, answer signals connected to `deleteLater`, a guarded late emit and a test hook. The helper should own it once, and the diff-map fetch that `DiffWidget` and `HunkDiffWidget` each write. About 90 minutes; an `emit_if_alive(signal, *args)` beside `start_daemon_thread` is a 20-minute first step, and the parentless sites above can adopt the helper as they are fixed.
- **One owner for a remote operation.** `_run_remote_op` takes four options and keeps its per-op state in five parallel fields, and each option adds a branch to a shared handler (the rebase added two). A `_RemoteOp` tuple (name, run, push target, undo target, cancellable) would carry them, and the 37 test references to the five fields in eight files move with it. About 90 minutes.
- **Settling a diff block in one place.** Retiring a skeleton is written three times (`diff.py`, `hunk_diff.py`, the viewport loader), and the generic loader imports `hunk_view` to do it. `HunkDiffWidget.load_all_files` is 70 lines doing two jobs. The cheap route is about 20 minutes; making the loader settle a block for both outcomes is 40 or more.
- **File-history membership as a query.** Which commits a file's history can hold is decided in the graph widget. About 75 minutes.
- **A typed ref badge.** Use a value type for a ref badge's kind instead of the `tag:` prefix and the `HEAD` sentinel. About 90 minutes.
- **One numbering helper for hunk lines.** Hunk line numbering is implemented once per renderer, so the marker fix had to land in both. About 20 minutes.
- **One save path for the repo list.** The list persists the store itself, alongside the window's save-and-report path. About 30 minutes.
- **Shared test builders.** A private `_git()` helper is copied into about 30 test modules, and real-repo, real-window and held-session builders are pasted into several new test files. About 45 minutes each.

## Smaller follow-ups the fixers noted

- **One tolerant remote listing.** The graph, the Create Tag dialog and the Remote Branches dialog each fall back on their own when `list_remotes` fails, and each reports it differently.
- **One identity check.** `WorkingTreeWidget._ensure_identity` is a second copy of `identity_dialog.ensure_identity`.
- **Pull without naming the remote.** When the branch has an upstream, Pull could run `git pull --rebase` with no arguments. This needs a port variant.
- **A stale Branches dialog.** An open Branches dialog keeps listing the previous repo's branches after a switch. Its Checkout is guarded, so it cannot act on the wrong repo.
- **A clearer remote delete prompt.** The prompt names `remote/branch` joined. Naming the two parts would read better when a remote's name contains '/'.
- **A muted notice that is not muted.** `make_overflow_notice`'s docstring says muted, but it sets no colour, so it draws in the default text colour; `on_surface_variant` would match the diff notice.
- **A bad text scale is only repaired on Apply.** `load_settings` stays raw, so a hand-edited non-numeric `typography_scale` is logged on each theme apply and each dialog open until the user presses Apply in Appearance (four times per launch in the real app, and nothing is shown in the UI). A number outside 0.5 to 2.0 is clamped without a warning, so `0` or `-3` gives a 5 pt UI. `avatar_gravatar_enabled` read from a hand-edited string `"false"` counts as true.
- **The Cancel tooltip.** It still says "Cancel the running push/pull/fetch", though Cancel also stops tag pushes, remote-branch deletes and the tag listing. Reword it when decision 3 is made.
- **Mixin docstrings.** The class docstrings of the other infrastructure mixins (`TagOps`, `RemoteOps`' lock and process attributes, the users of `_git_env`) still list only `self._repo`, though they rely on sibling methods; `BranchOps` now names all of theirs, but `DiffOps` names only `CommitOps`' `_diff_against_parent` and still omits `RepoStateOps`' `_git_env` and `SubmoduleOps`' `_detect_diverged_submodules`.
- **A dead fallback.** `_cancel_remote_op` falls back to the name "Remote operation", which cannot be reached: the running flag and the name are set and cleared together.

## Findings the verifiers disagreed on

These were left unchanged.

- **Repo-list maintenance in `main.py`.** The startup choice and the pruning of gone repos live in `main.py` against the concrete store. One verifier wanted an application use case; the other pointed out that `application/` holds only thin wrappers by design.
- **The log panel's row writers.** The two routines are near-identical. One verifier wanted them merged; the other found their essential step differs, insertHtml against formatted text that the recolouring reads.
- **The theme dialog's fill and repaint.** The pair appears at three sites and has a one-line alias.
- **The repo list's `show_open_repo_from` setter.** One verifier objected to its name and default; the other found the setter is always called before the first reload.
- **The Insight range math.** It lives in a dialog method, so its tests build a dialog. One verifier wanted a pure function; the other said the ranges are UI captions, not a domain concept.
- **The self-reference in `connect_widget`.** `theme/live.py` keeps a closure that names itself, so a destroyed widget's Python wrapper, and every parentless QObject hanging off it, lives until a garbage collection pass, which runs on whichever thread allocates most (the git workers). Both verifiers reproduced it; one wanted the 25-minute fix (a small listener class that lets go of the widget on `destroyed`), the other did not. They agree on the order: parent the sites in "Worker signals are still parentless" first. With the widget released at once, a worker that still holds a parentless signals object becomes its last owner and deletes it on the worker thread every time, instead of on whichever thread runs the collector.
- **How far a helper for thread-safe emits should go.** The one-background-worker-helper refactor above was split: one verifier wanted it designed together with the parentless sites, the other wanted only `emit_if_alive` now.
- **Shared helpers for the new tests.** `_settled` and `_quiet`, the refusal wait, and the notice finders: the verifiers split on whether three copies justify a shared module, and on whether to wait for a third user.
- **One helper for the "real `exec()`, reject from a timer, count what is left" test.** It is written three times (Appearance, the Worktrees dialog, the Checkout Branch dialog), and an older variant sits in the Insight dialog's tests. One verifier wanted a shared module now (about 25 minutes): a context manager that arms the timer, rejects the active modal widget and yields what it rejected, with the one non-obvious rule written once: the `exec()` must be the real one, because a stub skips what PySide does to a dialog it runs and every dialog then looks leaked. How the caller holds the dialog does not matter once the closures are gone. A probe on PySide6 6.12.0, the version `uv.lock` pins, left 0 under `w` for the Worktrees and Checkout Branch dialogs whether the opener ran `Dialog(parent=w).exec()`, held the dialog in a local, kept it in an attribute and cleared that afterwards, or called `del d` after `d.exec()`; before the fixes every opening left one more. The Theme dialog still has its closures, so it stays under the window whatever the caller does, and View > Appearance frees it only because `run_and_discard` deletes it. The other found the saving small (about 10 lines) and the hang the finding feared hypothetical: it needs the cancel helper itself to miss, and with their fixes reverted all three tests fail on their own assertion within a second. That verifier would wait for a fourth dialog test. If it is done, do not add an unbound second timer as a fallback (it outlives the test and can reject another test's dialog): bind a watchdog to the window, or add `pytest-timeout`.
