## Context

Round 3 of the code-review loop reviewed the 163 round-2 fix commits that shipped in v1.3.0 without a review of their own. Two independent verifiers checked every finding, and nothing below was fixed in that round.

Round 3 fixed everything that was confirmed in scope and estimated at 30 minutes or less:

- 62 correctness defects, each with a regression test. The review ran three passes, and each pass also checked the previous pass's fixes.
- 60 quality changes.
- One test fixture that crashed the interpreter. It fed mock data through typed Qt signals.

Round 4 (2026-11-07 to 2026-11-10) took the 18 defects round 3 had listed as found outside its scope. Three review passes then checked those fixes with the same method: reviewers by area, then two verifiers per finding. They confirmed 46, 24 and 10 problems in the fixes, and everything that both verifiers called worth fixing now was fixed. See [Round 4 update](#round-4-update).

Round 5 (2026-11-17 to 2026-11-18) fixed the worker signals that were still parentless in eight places; the Round 5 update replaces the bullet that listed them. See [Round 5 update](#round-5-update).

- Decisions only the maintainer can make.

## Needs a decision

1. **Conflicted hunks** still show a stage checkbox and a discard button that fail with a "hunk ... not found" error in the Operations Log. Hide them, or route them to whole-file staging?
2. **Push remote in fork setups.** Pull follows the branch's upstream; push still targets `origin`. Supporting `pushRemote` / `pushDefault` needs a new port.
3. **Cancelling an interactive rebase** midway: leave the repo REBASING, or abort? A plain rebase now runs in the remote-op slot without a Cancel button. The interactive rebase still shows one, and it stops nothing: a click logs "cancelling..." and the rebase ends as done a few seconds later. Its done row also has no Undo link, unlike the plain rebase's. Hide Cancel for it too, or make it stop the rebase?
4. **Localised git messages.** Run worktree commands under `LC_ALL=C` so the dirty and locked messages still parse?
5. **Graph toolbar overflow.** It is not clipped at the default width, but nothing handles a narrower one.
6. **What to block while a rebase runs.** Round 4 gated the rebase banner's Abort and Continue and the Local + remote button in the Delete tag prompt, and made the toolbar Push and Pull, which a detached HEAD already refused, say a rebase is running. Checkout, commit, reset and stash stay clickable, and a checkout in that window succeeds and makes the rebase fail. One guard shared by all of them, or a check inside each action, as Pull now has?
7. **The main checkout under a linked worktree's own row.** When a linked worktree has a row of its own, the list under it leaves out the main checkout (under the main repo's row, that checkout is the row). Should it be listed?

## Round 4 update

**Refuted: paging can skip commits.** The graph's `_load_more` worker does read `_extra_tips` when it runs, so a branch click in the middle of a page fetches a page of a different walk. But the click's reload sets `_reload_queued` in the same call, and `_on_append_done` drops the stale page before it touches the model, then reloads with the new tips. About 370 randomised interleavings appended nothing wrong; the only cost is one wasted walk. Optional hardening, about 10 minutes: capture `extra_tips` beside `skip` in `_load_more`.

**Not every `exec()` dialog leaks.** A reviewer suspected that Preferences, About, Merge, Reset, Interactive Rebase, Create tag, Identity and the other dialogs the app opens with `exec()` stay under the window until it closes. PySide6 6.12 hands a window-parented dialog back to Python inside the real `QDialog.exec()`, so a dialog survives only while something still references it: a closure that captures `self` and is connected to a signal of its own children, or the closure `connect_widget` connects to the theme manager's `theme_changed` (the Clone dialog is freed by `release()`, the Insight dialog by `WA_DeleteOnClose`). Of the dialogs listed, three had such a closure, and all three are now freed when they close: the Theme dialog (each View > Appearance opening left a dialog of about 150 widgets under the window that every later theme change restyled), the Worktrees dialog and the Checkout Branch dialog. A probe that stubs `exec()` says all of them leak, which is how the reviewer got there.

**The fixes were also run in the real application.** After the second review pass, four exercisers drove the real `main.main()` stack offscreen, each scenario in its own process with a temporary HOME and settings, over real temporary git repositories: linked worktrees, Ctrl+W and removing the worktree on screen; remote operations and the background rebase; the Clone, Add Worktree, Appearance, Worktrees and Checkout Branch dialogs; the diff panes, graph landing and blame. About 115 runs and 28 scenarios gave no crash, no traceback and no `MagicMock` line. Two scenarios failed, and both are items this document already lists as deferred: a RECENT row spelled another way survives a worktree removal, and a late failure of a repeated blame request for the same revision wipes the newer blame. Everything else the round fixed behaved as its tests say.

**What the later passes found in the round's own fixes.** Pass 1 confirmed 46 problems; they are fixed and visible in the history. The three worst of pass 2: Enter in the graph search on the already-current row did not cancel a pending branch landing; Ctrl+W between a worktree removal and the landing of its replacement wrote the deleted worktree into RECENT; and the viewport loader's parentless timer, freed by the garbage collector on a worker thread, killed some forward-order runs of the diff-pane test files in one process (exit 139) (the loader and its timer are now children of the scroll area). Pass 3 found that the repo list's Close item still did what that Ctrl+W did, because the guard sat in one of the two entries that reach `_on_repo_close`; it now sits where they meet. It also corrected the README's Ctrl+W row, which named how a worktree was opened where the code tests whether the worktree has a row of its own.

**What the evidence does not cover.** Every test run and every exerciser scenario ran offscreen on Windows, with macOS message-box titles simulated by a plugin. CI sets `QT_QPA_PLATFORM=offscreen` only in its Linux step, so its Windows and macOS jobs use the native platform plugins, and the new tests that run a dialog's real `exec()` open real windows there. Nothing was run on Linux or on a real macOS.

## Round 5 update

**Worker signals: the eight parentless sites are fixed, and the account of them needed five corrections.** A fresh check against `d09d3e41` reproduced all eight. Each site has a test that was written before its fix. Run against `d09d3e41`'s code in a scratch copy, 33 of the 39 tests in the series' seven new site-test files fail, and the other six pin what already held (a superseded load's late answer is dropped, and a worker that finishes after its window is gone raises nothing). The series adds an eighth new test file, `widgets/test_background.py`, for the two helpers; `d09d3e41` has no helpers, so it could not run there. Twelve of the 33 fail because a worker thread destroyed the signals object, 17 because nothing destroyed it on the GUI thread, three because `MainWindow` on `d09d3e41` has no `remote_op_runner` argument, and one times out waiting for a deletion that never comes. A sweep of every thread start and every worker `emit` under `git_gui/` and in `main.py` found no ninth site. The fix is two functions beside `start_daemon_thread` in `widgets/background.py`. `worker_signals` builds a worker's signals as a child of the `QApplication`, and `delete_when_done` wraps the worker so that its last act is `signals.deleteLater()`, which Qt carries out on the GUI thread after the answers the worker queued. Nothing a widget does can then delete the signals under a worker, so these sites need neither a `suppress` around their emits nor a `deleteLater` connected to each answer signal; the one case left is a worker that answers after the `QApplication` is destroyed (see the end of this section). The window's repo-ready signals take `worker_signals` but not `delete_when_done`: one object serves every repo open for the window's life, so it lives until the application exits.

What the first account of these sites, the bullet this update replaces, got wrong:

- **The graph is not the hottest site.** There is one `GraphWidget` for the life of the window, never replaced or destroyed; each load replaced its predecessor's signals object (`self._load_signals`). With real queries, a reload queued behind a running one freed the signals on a worker thread in none of 1,200 chains with the GUI thread idle and in 3 of 1,000 with it kept busy in Python (3 of 2,200 in all, about 0.14%); only zero-cost fake queries freed 1.4% to 9.6% of the signals objects. The sure way is a repo switch while a load is in flight: in one run of 100 switches with real queries, all 100 superseded loads were freed on a worker thread.
- **The hub's defect is not an owner being replaced.** `_repo_ready_signals` is one object for the window's life and nothing replaces it. It was parentless, and a dead window's Python wrapper kept it alive through a reference cycle (the repo list holds a lambda that names the window, `show_open_repo_from(lambda: self._repo_path)`) until a collection pass reached it. That pass runs on whichever thread allocates most, so in five runs over the 19 test files that read the window's repo-ready signals (121 windows built in each), 84 to 86 hubs were destroyed before the run ended and 7 to 34 of them on a worker thread. Only tests destroy a window before exit; at exit it was freed on the main thread (13 of 13 runs of a scratch probe that follows `main()`).
- **The remote operation's defect is a test-suite one.** The only chained operation, the force push offered after a rejected push, waits behind a modal message box. With a real box (clicked after 0 ms or 3 ms) and a 10 ms pause between chains, 0 of 400 chains freed the signals on a worker thread; with the box patched out, as the existing tests patch it, 87 of 200 did in the same loop. In a separate loop of 1,500 chains with a 2 ms pause between them, again with the box patched out, 775 of the 3,000 signals objects (two operations per chain) were freed on a worker thread.
- **Round 4's recipe was not the safe one.** The recipe parents a load's signals to the pane, connects every answer to `deleteLater` and wraps the emits in `suppress(RuntimeError)`. In a stress script (not in the repository; 50 processes of 5,000 iterations in each cell of a grid of two scenarios and two timing settings), the scenario that tears the owner down at the instant a worker emits crashed parentless signals (master) in 50 of 50 processes at both settings, round 4's recipe in 4 of 50 at the interpreter's default switch interval and in 49 of 50 once it was cut to 1 us with jitter, and signals owned by the application in 0 of 50 at both settings. With the owner kept alive, destroying parentless signals on a worker thread did not crash by itself when the answers went to a bound method of a live QObject, the shape of all eight sites: 250,000 such destructions in each of the two timing settings, 500,000 in all, without one crash. A slot that is not a bound method of a QObject is another matter. In a variant of the same scenario with the receiver changed (a scratch script, not in the repository), parentless signals destroyed on a worker thread crashed the process in every run when the answers went to a lambda (6 of 6 processes on PySide6 6.12.0, 3 of 3 more at a 1 us switch interval, 4 of 4 on 6.11.1) and, on 6.12.0, to one lambda shared by all of them, to a plain function or to a bound method of an object that is not a QObject (4 of 4 each), most of them within the first 1,000 iterations. When the answers went to a bound method of a QObject, defined on the widget or on a mixin of it, none of 17 processes crashed (14 on 6.12.0, three of those at a 1 us switch interval), and each destroyed all 5,000 of its signals objects on a worker thread. A lambda did not crash either when the GUI thread dropped the last reference, so that the free happened there (0 of 8). All eight sites connect `self._on_...` bound methods, the safe shape. So the reflog pane, the Insight dialog and the Add Worktree dialog, the owners a user closes under a running worker, had a crash path; the graph, the sidebar, the remote operations and the hub were a breach of Qt's contract and of the test suite's hygiene.
- **The reflog pane's two `waitSignal` tests need no change.** The old bullet said that once the signals are deleted with `deleteLater`, such a wait can lose its wake-up and the tests should use `wait_until_deleted`. The worker asks for the deletion after its last answer, so a wait on the signals does not lose it: 120 of 120 runs of the two tests at the last commit of the series, in four loops of 30 that ran at the same time.

Three more things came out of the work:

- `ReflogModel()` was parentless as well. Of the owners of a parentless model, the reflog pane is the one a user closes in normal use, so its model was freed by whichever thread ran the collector. It is `ReflogModel(parent=self)` now (by keyword: the first parameter is `entries`).
- In PySide6 6.12.0, the locked version, `deleteLater()` on a parentless, Python-owned QObject hands its ownership to Qt, so `delete_when_done` alone frees such signals on the GUI thread too (30 processes of 5,000 iterations: no crash, and none of the 150,000 signals objects destroyed off the GUI thread). A probe on 6.11.1, the oldest version `pyproject.toml` allows, behaved the same. The application as parent is kept anyway: it does not lean on that PySide behaviour, and the hub, which is never handed to `delete_when_done`, needs it to live until the application exits. `test_letting_go_of_the_signals_does_not_delete_them` pins it for the helper and `test_the_signals_outlive_their_window_whichever_thread_collects_its_wrapper` for the hub; the tests of the other sites cannot tell the two apart.
- The hub is the application's, not the window's. The first version of its fix made it a child of the window, as round 4 did for the diff panes. An emit storm (four threads emitting in a tight loop while the window was destroyed, 200 windows per process, 10 processes) killed 10 of 10 processes with an access violation on that version, and none of 10 with the application as the owner or on master. Each window a test builds therefore leaves one idle `_RepoReadySignals` under the application until the session ends; the shipped app builds one window. A full run of the suite at the last commit of the series ended with 370 children under the application: 345 repo-ready signals (344 `_RepoReadySignals`, and one `Recording` that a test substitutes for the class), one for each of the 345 windows the tests built; 17 signals of workers that tests never ran (listed under Tests below); and 8 objects that are not signals, Qt's own and plain `QObject`s.

**What the evidence does not cover.** Every run was offscreen on Windows; nothing ran on Linux or macOS, and none against a real dead network share. The real `main()` was launched only in a throwaway profile (a fresh `USERPROFILE`, `APPDATA` and `LOCALAPPDATA`, headless, a scenario driven from its event loop): the 21-step scenario passed at the tip of each of the series' five pull requests (the launcher prints 23 records: its isolation check, the 21 steps and a verdict). It opens the reflog pane and reloads it, walks the Insight dialog through every range and closes it mid-walk, types a location into Add Worktree and cancels, reloads the graph and the sidebar, fetches from a local origin twice, and switches between two repositories, once with two switches racing. Each time the developer's real profile (its `.gitcrisp` folder, the app's AppData folders and its registry key) was unchanged and the log held no traceback or `RuntimeError`. Twelve in-process scenarios, some with a load held while its owner is closed or replaced, destroyed none of the 116 signals objects their sites created on a worker thread on the final tree (104 of them on the GUI thread; the 12 repo-ready signals objects live until exit); on master the same scenarios created 106 and destroyed 20 of them on a worker thread (the final tree creates 10 more because Add Worktree now makes one signals object per location check, not one per dialog). A worker that answers after the `QApplication` is destroyed raises `RuntimeError: Signal source has been deleted` on master and after these changes alike (PySide invalidates every Python-owned QObject when the application goes); the release build's Sentry setup has its default logging and threading integrations, so it may report that, and this was not checked.

## Defects found outside the round's scope

Each was reported by a reviewer or a fixer in round 4, except two that were reported in the descriptions of the later fixes [#421](https://github.com/Aidan79225/GitCrisp/pull/421) and [#422](https://github.com/Aidan79225/GitCrisp/pull/422) (the change-detector freeze under Threads and object lifetime, and the shutdown abort under Tests), both already there before those fixes, and six that round 5 found while fixing the worker signals (the parented signals whose emits are not guarded, the four parentless models, the context menus, the remote-tag cache write, Add Worktree's location check, and the idle signals objects the tests leave). Most were reproduced in a scratch run; none has a failing test in the repository.

**Threads and object lifetime**

- **A pane destroyed while a worker is inside `emit()` can crash the process.** Since round 4 a load's signals are children of the pane, so destroying the pane in the instant a worker emits deletes the object the worker is calling into. A stress probe on the real `HunkDiffWidget` ended in an access violation at the emit in 3 of its runs. An emit of the same shape sits in `hunk_diff.py` twice, `working_tree.py` twice and `diff.py` once; the blame pane has the same shape and, unlike the diff panes, is closed in normal use. The suite does not show it because the window is the length of one emit. Parenting the signals to `QApplication.instance()` instead closes it (the connection dies with the pane, a late emit finds a live object with no receiver, `deleteLater` still frees it on the GUI thread, and the `suppress` can go, except against a worker that answers after the application is destroyed, which still raises `RuntimeError`; about 40 minutes). Round 4 kept the `suppress` and documents the residual in the code. A separate stress run on the blame pane, closing it at random instants around the emit, survived 1,500 to 6,000 closes at each of three sizes (500, 5,000 and 100,000 lines) without a crash or a warning, so the window is narrow. Round 5 measured the recipe in a scratch stress script (not in the repository; 50 processes of 5,000 iterations, the owner torn down at the instant of the emit): round 4's recipe crashed 4 of 50 processes at the interpreter's default timing and 49 of 50 once its switch interval was cut to 1 us with jitter added, and signals owned by the application, as `worker_signals` and `delete_when_done` make them, 0 of 50 in both settings. The six round-4 sites (the blame pane, the Clone dialog and four diff-pane paths) can adopt the two helpers.
- **Parented signals whose emits are not guarded.** `_run_worktree_op` (in `repo_lifecycle.py`, two emits) and the Remote Branches and Submodule dialogs emit from a worker into a signals object that their owner parents, with no `suppress(RuntimeError)`; `_GoneRepoPruner` in `main.py` and `UpdateChecker` emit a signal of their own, and their owner parents them. None can free its signals from a worker, so none is the defect round 5 fixed. But a worker that emits after its owner is gone raises `RuntimeError` in the worker (the thread excepthook in `logging_setup.py` logs it), and an owner deleted in the middle of an emit has the window of the bullet above. `worker_signals` and `delete_when_done` would fit the first three as they are; the other two would need a small signals object of their own.
- **Four of the window's models are parentless.** `GraphModel` (`graph.py:484`), `DiffModel` (`diff.py:220`) and the `QStandardItemModel`s of the sidebar (`sidebar.py:181`) and the repo list (`repo_list.py:355`) are built without a parent, as `ReflogModel` was. Only tests destroy a window before exit. A dead window's Python wrapper, and those of the widgets under it, are kept alive by reference cycles until a collection pass (the window's, described in the Round 5 update, and each widget's `connect_widget` closure, described under the disagreements below), which leaves the models to the garbage collector. In three runs at `d09d3e41`, where the hub was parentless too, over the 19 test files that read the window's repo-ready signals (121 windows built in each; a scratch tracking plugin, not in the repository), 84, 85 and 84 of the hubs were destroyed before the run ended, and each of the four models showed the same counts as the hub: 8, 9 and 11 of them destroyed on a thread other than the GUI thread. Nothing crashed. Passing the owner as the parent, as `ReflogModel(parent=self)` does, is one line each; about 20 minutes with a test per model.
- **A context menu is left behind by every right-click that opens one.** All eight context menus (the sidebar's, the graph's, the repo list's, the diff's, the blame pane's, the file navigator's, the reflog pane's and the working tree's) are built as `QMenu(widget)`, run with `exec` and never deleted, so N right-clicks that open one leave N menus under the widget (the reflog pane's and the working tree's under a view inside it) until it is destroyed. In a scratch probe with the real `QMenu.exec` and a timer that closed the popup, 3 calls of each menu's handler left 3 children under each of the eight, and 5 right-clicks on each of a sidebar branch, remote-branch, stash and tag row left 5 each. A right-click that opens no menu leaves none: the sidebar builds one only for branch, remote-branch, stash and tag rows, and the graph, the reflog pane, the working tree, the file navigator and the blame pane return before building one when there is no row or line to act on (the graph also on its working-tree row). The repo list is the exception: it builds its `QMenu(self)` before it checks the row and returns without opening it when the row has no menu, which leaves an empty one.
- **`JsonRemoteTagCache.save` writes its file in place** on the remote-operation worker (`_update_remote_tag_cache`, and the delete-tag operation) while the sidebar worker may be inside `load` for the same repository. A read between the truncate and the write sees an empty file or, rarely, a truncated one (in a scratch probe only for a cache of 20,000 tags, about 190 KB, or more), takes the `JSONDecodeError` branch and returns `{}`, so the remote-tag icons vanish until the operation's own completion reload, a moment later (for a tag deleted on several remotes, until the last remote is done). In six scratch loops of `load` against `save` (three on the real class, three on copies of its steps), with the reader and the writer running flat out, 45% to 78% of the loads returned `{}`; with a timer asking the sidebar to reload every 3 ms during 80 simulated fetches (the app's own remote-operation code, the git call replaced by a 30 ms sleep), 1 of the 376 cache loads did, and 160 of those 376 were an operation's own read and its completion reload, which run before and after its write. Writing to a temporary file in the same folder and replacing the file with `os.replace` ends the torn read, but on Windows `os.replace` raises `PermissionError` while a reader has the file open, `load` turns its own `PermissionError` into `{}` as well, and `_update_remote_tag_cache` swallows the writer's. So a fix needs more than the rename: in the flat-out loop that tried the options (5, 200 and 2,000 tags), `os.replace` alone left 20% to 31% of the loads at `{}`, adding a retry on `PermissionError` in `save` 4% to 12%, and a lock that `load` and `save` share 0%.
- **A failed `Thread.start()` leaves state set.** `remote_op_queue.py` sets `_remote_running` before it starts its thread, so a start that fails wedges the remote slot for the session; `repo_lifecycle.py` leaves a repo request pending; `graph.py` sets `_loading` before it starts either load thread, so every later reload and page load is turned away until a repo switch; the Remote Branches and Submodule dialogs go busy before they start their threads, and a busy dialog refuses to close. Latent: it needs the OS to refuse a thread, when every worker fails anyway. About 10 minutes each (reset in an `except`, or set the state after `start()`). The Clone dialog was fixed. Since round 5 a worker whose thread never starts also leaves its signals object, an idle child of the application, until exit: the helpers delete it only when the worker returns.
- **On Windows the change detector can still freeze the app while it is built.** [#421](https://github.com/Aidan79225/GitCrisp/pull/421) fixed the same freeze in `RepoChangeDetector.stop()` by deleting the watcher, which lets go of the GIL while it waits. It did not fix `__init__`, which adds each watch path with its own `addPath` call: `addPath` keeps the GIL while it waits for the lock that Qt's watcher thread holds when it logs about a deleted directory. PySide6 takes the GIL before it calls the app's Python message handler (`_qt_message_filter` in `main.py`), so the main thread and Qt's watcher thread wait for each other, and a watched directory deleted while the `addPath` calls run can freeze the app until it is killed (checked on Qt 6.12.0 and PySide6 6.12.0). The calls take a few milliseconds together on a local disk (a median of 2.3 to 2.5 ms for the whole `__init__` of a linked worktree of this project), which should keep this rare. The case #421 names is removing the linked worktree on screen from outside the app while its detector is built. That worktree's own git directory is added first, but `git worktree remove` deletes the rest of the worktree before it ([#422](https://github.com/Aidan79225/GitCrisp/pull/422)), so the `addPath` calls have to still be running when git gets there. In two repeats of a scratch sweep against a real `git worktree remove` (git 2.54; not in the repository), with start delays chosen around git's deletion of the worktree's `.git` file, which `__init__` reads first, `__init__` hung in 16 of 200 runs with an empty worktree and 7 of 160 with 30 files, but in none of 128 with 200 files, where git needs about 17 ms between deleting that file and deleting the worktree's git directory. These counts show that it can happen, not how often: the sweeps' `__init__` probably took about 8 ms (it did on a worktree made moments before), not the 2.3 to 2.5 ms above. `.git/worktrees`, which git deletes with the last linked worktree, is added last, so by the order of the calls alone no call is left for its deletion to catch. No freeze in real use has been traced to this, but a frozen app logs nothing, so the app's log file could not show one. Keeping Qt's log call away from the Python handler while the paths are added would remove this freeze too (#421), but that is a process-wide change, and clearing the handler for that time with `qInstallMessageHandler(None)` can kill the whole process if a Qt thread is already waiting for the GIL to call it (#422). Not estimated.

**Graph**

- **A failed reload leaves its state behind.** `_on_load_failed` keeps `_pending_scroll_oid` and `_extra_tips`. After a failed click on a branch, a later bare reload (the focus-return one) lands on that branch's commit, and a later `_load_more` pages with the new tips and the old skip.
- **A branch click loads one page more than it needs and lands at the bottom edge.** The landing scrolls its target to the top while the scroll bar still has the old range, so `_on_scroll` asks for another page (`_land_on_pending_target` → `scroll_to_oid` → `_load_more`): three graph loads instead of two, 150 rows instead of 100, and the commit sits at the bottom of the viewport. Harmless to correctness.
- **Two short-lived oddities around a pending landing.** While its doubling reload runs, the commit picked earlier has no current row in the list although the diff pane still shows it (one load; longer on a large repo). A search typed while a landing is pending shows the branch tip's diff for a moment before the match takes over; the final state is right.
- **Dropping a landing can lose the picked commit.** When a pick of a drawn commit supersedes a pending branch landing, the load already running comes back as a plain first-round load. If the branch's own commits push the picked commit past the first page, `_restore_view_state` cannot find it and clears the selection while the diff pane still shows the commit. Rare: it needs a branch with more than a page of new commits. Fix: keep the doubling rounds with a "keep this commit drawn" goal; about 45 minutes.

**Remote operations and rebase**

- **Push and Pull after a rebase stops on a conflict give the wrong advice.** HEAD is detached and the repo is REBASING, so the Operations Log says "HEAD is not on a branch (detached, or no commits yet). Check out a branch first"; it should say to continue or abort the rebase.
- **A plain rebase that stops on a conflict is logged as an error.** The row is red, reads "ERROR: Rebasing (1/3) ... hint: ..." with git's raw progress and hint lines (including a `git rebase --skip` hint the UI has no button for), and force-expands the log, although a conflict stop is a normal state with the Rebase in progress banner up.
- **The Rebase in progress banner is unreliable while a background rebase runs.** Seen in the real app with a slow hook: with the same setup the banner came up within half a second in 4 of 9 runs and not at all until the rebase ended (or F5) in the other 5; the working-tree commit button also kept reading "Commit". Abort and Continue are refused while the slot is busy anyway, so only the display lags. A candidate cause, not proven: `RepoChangeDetector` drops watcher events for 500 ms after the window reloads itself (`repo_change_detector.py`, `notify_self_reload`), and the rebase writes nothing else under `.git` during a long hook.
- **Rebase Continue and Abort, and the other local git actions, still run on the GUI thread.** Continue replays the rest of the todo list and freezes the window the way a plain rebase did before it moved to the remote-op slot. Merge, cherry-pick, revert, reset and checkout have the same freeze class; not measured.
- **Re-opening the active repo mid-rebase loses the rebase's Undo.** A click on the active repo's row is ignored, but opening it again (the Open dialog on its folder, Open in the Worktrees dialog, or the Open entry on a linked worktree's row) rebuilds the session buses, and the rule that ties an Undo to its buses then gives that rebase a plain "done" line. A path-based check would restore the link.
- **The Delete tag prompt reads the remote-op slot once.** If the running operation ends while the prompt is open, "Local + remote" stays disabled and the text goes stale. **Worktrees and repo lifecycle**

- **Deleting the branch checked out in the worktree on screen leaves the window on the dead path.** `branch_flows._remove_worktree_for_branch_delete` removes the worktree synchronously on the GUI thread and never reaches `_on_worktree_removed`, so the window keeps showing the folder and its change detector keeps watching it. Read from code and probed; about 45 to 60 minutes.
- **More worktree-removal gaps.** A reload already in flight, or an F5 during the removal, still runs `git status` in the worktree; stopping the detector only removes the reloads it schedules. A window opened on a subfolder of the worktree (`W/src`) is matched by neither the removal fix nor the repo list's text comparison. `forget()` receives only the open row's stored spelling: a RECENT or active entry spelled like git's, next to an open row spelled otherwise, is not forgotten in the same session, and the start-up prune handles it at the next launch. - **More GUI-thread disk reads that could hit a dead share.** `repo_lifecycle.py` (`Path(candidate).is_dir()` when it chooses the repo to show after a removal), `right_panel.py` (`os.path.exists` on `<path>/.git`) and the change detector's `exists` / `is_dir` on watched paths; that last one runs on the GUI thread too: the window builds the detector there (at start-up, whenever a repo opens, and when a failed worktree removal puts the watch back) and the detector makes those calls in its constructor. - **Add Worktree's location check catches only `OSError`.** If `_disk_problem` raised anything else, its worker would die without answering: the "Checking location" hint would stay and Add would stay disabled until the text changed. - **Ctrl+W or Close on a removed worktree's row after a click elsewhere.** The round's guard covers the time between a worktree removal and the landing of its replacement. - **The log panel's row writers.** The two routines are near-identical. One verifier wanted them merged; the other found their essential step differs, insertHtml against formatted text that the recolouring reads.
- **The theme dialog's fill and repaint.** The pair appears at three sites and has a one-line alias.

- **The Insight range math.** It lives in a dialog method, so its tests build a dialog. One verifier wanted a pure function; the other said the ranges are UI captions, not a domain concept.
- **The self-reference in `connect_widget`.** `theme/live.py` keeps a closure that names itself, so a destroyed widget's Python wrapper, and every parentless QObject hanging off it, lives until a garbage collection pass, which runs on whichever thread allocates most (the git workers). Round 5 took that first step: the signals of those sites belong to the application, seven are deleted on the GUI thread when their own worker returns, and the window's repo-ready signals live until the application exits, so letting go of a widget at once no longer makes a worker the last owner of anything it answers through.

- **Shared helpers for the new tests.** `_settled` and `_quiet`, the refusal wait, and the notice finders: the verifiers split on whether three copies justify a shared module, and on whether to wait for a third user.
- **One helper for the "real `exec()`, reject from a timer, count what is left" test.** It is written three times (Appearance, the Worktrees dialog, the Checkout Branch dialog), and an older variant sits in the Insight dialog's tests.
